# Quality or Cringe

Generally speaking, most people can agree that there's things out there that make us laugh, and things that make us cringe.&#x20;

This article is a low quality take from an chronically online engineer who makes some of these (I hope) funnier memes and sketches about where the line between the 2 is. I don't want this article to be taken as a completely serious take, as what's the point of humor if we boil it down to a science? I'm sure there's some psychologist out there working on this sort of thing, but this article definintely isn't that.

While there's humor that straddles the line between funny and cringe as a bit of a meta-catagory of humor, this article discusses the general base cases that fit firmly across on different sides.&#x20;

### Successories

During my time at a company with older office decor from the 90s, there were a good amount of posters around the office with these (from a modern perspective) relatively cringey motivational quotes. A lot of these had high quality images framed and intended to be taken at face value without any sense of sarcasm or humor.&#x20;

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (6).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

These posters are created by a company known as "Successories", a self proclaimed "_leader in motivational and recognition products and programs_". However, anybody who's been on the internet in the early 2000s knows the next part of the story; the format becomes so oversaturated due to its prevalence in corporate offices, that it takes on a 2nd "ironic" form that pokes fun at its own format.&#x20;

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (4).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The ironic part is that this then wraps in on itself: the meme format itself becomes oversaturated in itself and the lack of understanding where the format comes from turns what was cool into a cringe over-used format. The "demotivational poster" format becomes a hallmark of the early 2010s, but quickly dies off as the "demotivational" format just isn't that funny to look at. As the "Successories" posters leave offices nation-wide the meme format becomes more and more obscure and nobody seems to understand why it was funny in the first place.

That being said, the existence of temporarily funny and restrictive formats implies that there exists formats that are funny and transcend generations, and this article intends to capture one of these.

### Look Back and Cringe

I'm going to assault your eyes for a second here (so sorry):

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (8).png" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

There is a subset of the population that unironically believes this image looks good and is perfectly fine to post on Linkedin as part of a jab at . However to the average person it's unbelievably cringe, but why? Is there a reason we seem to have an allergic reaction to content like this? Is it an unwarranted knee-jerk reaction?&#x20;

I say no, this feeling of unease and immediate repulsiveness is a good sign that most people won't stand for slop content. It maintains general standards across the internet and keeps innovation high.&#x20;

To put it into perspective, both the successories motivational format and your average AI generated slop both:

1. Are low quality templatized image formats intended for high-volume production.
2. Follow uncanny patterns of detail, yet are somehow enjoyed by a certain subset of the population.
3. Are incredibly sanitized with no real messaging other than generalized theming.
4. Low effort to produce, ranging from filling in pre-existing text or prompting AI image generation.

This slots both types of formats into that "slop" or "soulless" category. It creates that immediate "allergic reaction" in most people with taste when they see it. People don't want to consume media or art that doesn't have any real intention behind it other than to be another boxed meal coming out of an assembly line.

Personally, I think what's even more concerning than the slop formats themselves are the types of people that take joy in this type of these mass-manufactured memes. They objectively have bad taste and their lack of drive to consume something better points to societal complacency (completely self aware this sounds incredibly harsh). It's the same mentality that allows the elderly population on facebook to find minion memes continously funny despite it being a relatively stagnant format with nowhere to evolve.

### The "Quality" MS Paint Scribble

A certain art-form has always been prevalent in a time of AI generated mass-produced slop. Something expressing nothing but soul with its hand-crafted and bound-less nature: **The MS-paint Scribble**.&#x20;

Being by definition hand crafted, many variations of this format exist. Wojak, Pepe, and other early 2000s staples stem from originally being hand-drawn creations that have been hand-modified to fit new eras and more recent events, the most notable being Pepe being used in a relatively mainstream way by conservatives during the 2016 elections.

Interestingly enough, the hand-drawn meme format is also self-culling. Certain variations of the wojak and the "troll face" meme from the early internet seem to sometimes pop in and die out quickly as people don't find the "mass produced" versions of them as funny as the ones that are hand drawn to fit specific people or situations.

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (10).png" alt="" width="349"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The variation I want to talk about today isn't one of these, but more of a generalized "re-drawing" of pre-existing logos or pieces of art that originally had more serious intent.

Immediately off the top of my head was the VEX-U logo being turned into a MS-paint scribble as the unofficial logo for an event in 2021 unofficially known as the "**Quality In Person Event**" (QIPE) during a time when many states were starting to lift their covid restrictions for in person events. With a representative accidentally naming the event by stating they were going to have a "Quality In Person Event" the collegiate robotics community decided to create a "Quality" logo.

At the end of the day, the practice of taking something pre-established and parodying it is an art-form that won't ever grow old. Ironically though, the way to keep these funny is to _not_ pump out and mass-manufacture them. What seperates a funny scribble or meme from a mass-manufactured piece of slop comes down to what is reflected in the message behind the image being sent and its context.

**The same joke can only be told so many times until original author intent is called into question and the absurdity of the humor is lost.**
